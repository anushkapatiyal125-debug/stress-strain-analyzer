    Theory and formulas used

    Engineering vs true values

    σ = F/A₀, ε = ΔL/L₀. True stress σT = σ(1+ε) and true strain εT = ln(1+ε), valid up to the start of necking.

    Modulus of elasticity

    Hooke's law σ = Eε. E is the slope of the linear part, found by least-squares regression. It reflects the stiffness of the interatomic bonds.

    0.2% offset yield

    A line of slope E is drawn from ε = 0.002. Where it cuts the curve is the yield strength σy, i.e. the stress that leaves 0.2% permanent strain.

    Ultimate tensile strength

    UTS = Fmax/A₀, the peak of the engineering curve. Necking begins here (Considère: dσT/dεT = σT, so εT at UTS ≈ n).

    % Elongation

    %EL = (Lf − L₀)/L₀ × 100 = (εf − σf/E) × 100. The elastic strain σf/E springs back after fracture, so it is subtracted.

    Energy

    Modulus of resilience Ur = σy²/2E (energy stored elastically). Toughness = ∫σ dε, the whole area under the curve. 1 MPa = 1 MJ/m³.

    Schmid's law

    Resolved shear stress τR = σ cosφ cosλ. Slip starts when τR reaches τCRSS. Slip planes at 45° to the load have the maximum Schmid factor, 0.5.

    Strain hardening

    Dislocations multiply (Frank–Read sources) and block each other. Taylor relation: σ = σ₀ + MαGb√ρ, where ρ is dislocation density (≈10¹⁰ → 10¹⁴ m⁻²).

    Grain boundaries and yield point

    Hall–Petch: σy = σ₀ + k d−½. In mild steel, C and N atoms pin dislocations (Cottrell atmospheres), giving an upper and lower yield point and Lüders bands.
